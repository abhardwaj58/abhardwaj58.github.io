Projects

Thyra

Healthcare AI · Personal Project

A triage routing tool built to explore whether AI could automate the first decision in healthcare.

Not diagnosis. The decision before that. Do you need a doctor on a screen, a telehealth consult, or an ambulance? In India, that decision is often made by whoever picks up the phone, with no clinical logic behind it. RMP networks are the de facto first contact for hundreds of millions of people. Thyra was an attempt to give that first contact a smarter decision layer. My mom is a biochemist. My sister contributed clinical escalation logic. This was my first application. Built with Lovable, Supabase, and Claude.

Key decision: The routing logic came from family, not documentation. That is how it should work.

GitHub Lovable · Supabase · Claude

Panorama

Clinical AI · Healthcare

A two-layer clinical oversight platform built for physician supervisors managing home-based NP workflows.

The real problem was not missing data. It was cognitive overload. Dr. Chen supervises 30+ charts a month with 15 minutes to do it. She is legally liable for every missed contraindication. I designed a workflow where she clears her queue in the time she actually has. Two-layer risk detection: SQL for known contraindications, deterministic, must fire 100% of the time. Claude Haiku for free-text NP note analysis, catching what structured fields miss. Ran a live clinical usability session with a physician with 20+ years of experience. All four roadmap priorities 3 through 6 came from that single session.

Key decision: SQL for rules, AI for language. The roles do not overlap.

Live site GitHub Lovable · Supabase · Claude Haiku · TypeScript

Wealth Agent

Agentic AI · Personal Project

A portfolio management agent built for my dad, who does not trust what he cannot understand.

My dad manages his own investments. He reads balance sheets, tracks positions, runs his own research. He just does it slowly. I built an agent that does the research layer for him: reads financial data, surfaces signals, explains reasoning in plain language. What started as a father-daughter project became a real experiment in agentic architecture. I tested different model choices, tuned for token consumption, and learned firsthand how much cost depends on how you structure prompts and chain tools. The real insight was not technical. Adoption does not happen because something is smart. It happens because someone trusts it.

Key decision: Explanation is not optional. If my dad cannot follow the reasoning, the agent has failed.

Live site GitHub Python · Streamlit

How I Think

I grew up in Himachal Pradesh, where two million people in the foothills of the Himalayas lack access to basic diagnostic testing. My mother is a biochemist. The idea of building the first accredited diagnostic lab in that region has been in my plans since before my MBA. That is not a professional ambition. It is a moral position: that access to healthcare information should not be determined by geography or wealth.

At Abbott I spent six years learning that the instrument always works. What fails is the human system around it. The nurse who does not trust the result. The lab director who was not consulted. The administrator who cannot see the ROI. I have spent my career in that gap. At HiLabs I stabilized a Fortune 30 client account that was in active escalation. The AI was fine. The relationship had broken. Same problem, different stack.

During the COVID crisis in India, a friend's father was dying and there were no hospital beds available. I was volunteering with Inaara, matching patients with plasma donors. We expanded to track beds, oxygen, and resources in real time. The platform connected over 900 patients with critical resources. My friend's father still passed away. I kept going. That instinct, building a parallel system when the existing one fails, shows up in everything I do. I am also a writer, a former college editor, and someone who finds genuine joy in the hidden architecture of language. At BITS I built an anonymous submissions section in the student magazine so students who were afraid to speak under their names could still be heard. Creating space for trust to form is not a professional skill. It is just how I operate.
