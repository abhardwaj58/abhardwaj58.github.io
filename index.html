Clinical AI · Healthcare

A two-layer clinical oversight platform built for physician supervisors managing home-based NP workflows.

Built for Sprinter Health's take-home assessment. The real problem wasn't missing data — it was physician cognitive overload. I designed a workflow where Dr. Chen clears 30+ charts in 15 minutes. Wealth Agent

Agentic AI · Personal Project

A portfolio management agent built for my dad, who does not trust what he cannot understand.

My dad manages his own investments. He reads balance sheets, tracks positions, runs his own research. He just does it slowly. I built this as an agent that does the research layer for him — reads financial data, surfaces signals, explains reasoning in plain language. What started as a father-daughter project became an experiment in model architecture: I tested different model choices, tuned for token consumption, and learned firsthand how much agentic cost depends on how you structure the prompts and chain the tools.

The real insight: Adoption does not happen because something is smart. It happens because someone trusts it.

How I Think

AI deployment fails at the human layer, not the technical one. The model is rarely the problem. The operator who does not trust it, the clinician who ignores its flags, the sales team that cannot explain it — those are the failure points. I have spent a decade working in exactly that gap.

At Abbott, I deployed point-of-care diagnostics across 200+ hospital clients in 30+ countries. The instrument always worked. What determined success was whether the nurse trusted it, whether the lab director felt heard, whether the hospital administrator saw the ROI. Same problem, different stack. At HiLabs, I stabilized a Fortune 30 client that was RED. The AI was fine. The relationship had broken.

I am also a writer, a former college editor, and someone who finds genuine joy in the hidden architecture of language. I built an anonymous submissions section at my university magazine so students who were afraid to speak under their names could still be heard. That instinct — creating space for trust to form — shows up everywhere I work.
